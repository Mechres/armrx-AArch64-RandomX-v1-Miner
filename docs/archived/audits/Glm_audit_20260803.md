## 1. Executive Summary

armrx is a mature, clean-room AArch64 RandomX v1 miner at **functional parity with XMRig** on the target device (MSM8929 / 8× Cortex-A53 @ 765 MHz, no cpufreq). Project state as of HEAD (`0e72144`):

- **Per-core: armrx BEATS XMRig.** 5.11 vs 5.04 H/s (cross build + E24).
- **8-worker: armrx is 95.2% of XMRig** (26.65 vs 28 H/s, real pool, 1209 s run), with the residual 4.8% gap *re-located* by the most recent measurement (M1, commit `15a88f0`, 2026-08-03) to **instruction-count/IPC, not `*_M` load-stall** as previously hypothesized.
- The codebase exhibits **exemplary measurement discipline** — 14+ dead leads documented, every claim gated by KATs + stress suites, every measurement carries toolchain/clock-consistency caveats.
- The two largest wins in project history were both *operational/toolchain* (not code): `isolcpus` (+14% H/s) and **GCC 16.1.0 cross-build (+7.9%)**. The largest *code* win was **E24** (+7.1% H/s) — counter-intuitively *adding* an instruction to break the A53 MAC interlock.
- The code-level optimization surface is largely exhausted. Three structural levers remain (in order of potential): the CBRANCH replay-map redesign, Clang cross-build A/B, and a "JIT-emit vs XMRig-emit" systematic per-opcode diff.
- Two latent issues found by this audit: a **likely-broken SIGINT handler** (pool-mining Ctrl-C bug, confirmed by user) and a **stale README/RETROSPECTIVE H/s baseline** (still quotes pre-E24 24.95 H/s).

**Bottom line:** This is no longer a "find the bottleneck" project. It is a "accept 95.2% as parity OR commit to a multi-day infrastructure investment" project. Audit verdicts in §3–§5 reflect this.

## 3. Critical Performance Bottlenecks (current state, post-M1)

Ranked by *current evidence-backed impact on the residual 4.8% gap*:

### 3.1 **JIT-emitted instruction-count excess in the main-VM body** (HEAD's #1 open lever)
**Status:** Confirmed by M1 (commit `15a88f0`).
- armrx emits 103.5M instr/hash vs XMRig 98.9M (Δ +4.6M, +4.6%) at IPC 0.598 vs 0.609.
- The +4.6% instruction-count vs +4.8% throughput gap is nearly 1:1 — the gap is now *primarily an emission question*, not stalls.
- Region census (W1-1): superscalar 80.5% of instr (95.77M, IPC 0.800) / main-VM JIT 9.9% (IPC 0.405) / AES+glue 9.5%. **The main-VM IPC 0.405 is the worst region**, but M1 says per-region stall attribution is not the differentiator — total stall count is ~equal. So the lever is *what* armrx emits per VM opcode, not *how it stalls*.
- The most concrete sub-investigation: E3b "main-VM memory-op emission ordering vs XMRig" (`perf-tracking.md:574`) is **OPEN** but has not been executed.

### 3.2 The CBRANCH replay invariant (architectural ceiling for `*_M` optimization)
**Status:** Mechanism fully root-caused by Gemini audit (`docs/audits/gemini_full-audit_2026-08-05.md` §C). The invariant is:
> For every pair of adjacent VM instructions in the CBRANCH replay window, their emitted byte blocks must be contiguous and non-overlapping. **No bytes from instruction `i+1` may be emitted within instruction `i`'s byte range.**

- Any cross-handler byte-relocation (E26 hoist, W3-2 scheduler extension) **structurally** violates this; both attempts SEGFAULT/diverge.
- The only safe path forward is **building an instruction-index-to-byte-offset map** during emission and rewriting `h_CBRANCH` to look up the replay target from the map rather than `reg_changed_offset`. This is the k3 plan (commit `0e72144` "M1→V→C1→D").
- **Cost/benefit is unfavorable for the residual 1.35 H/s gain** on an at-parity miner (Gemini's verdict: "multi-day implementation with non-trivial correctness risk").

### 3.3 Multi-worker scaling / weak-cluster contention (system-level, ~33% of per-core potential)
**Status:** Architectural — SoC design (MSM8929 two-cluster, cores 4–7 lose ~50% throughput to interconnect arbitration). 
- armrx 1w→8w scales 5.4× (8×4.08 ideal → 22 actual = 67% of linear); XMRig 6.1×. The ~33% loss is structural; both miners bear it, armrx slightly worse for unresolved causes.
- Not a software cap; `mining_engine.cpp:367` pins `AffinityMode::All` 1:1 to `core_order_`, which is correct on this device (no-cpufreq fallback coincides with the right split). E15 ruled out affinity as the cause.

### 3.4 The 3-cycle A53 integer L1 load-use latency ( фундаментальный floor)
**Status:** Architectural property of the in-order A53 pipeline (TRM 3-cycle L1 int load-use). M1 shows XMRig pays the same tax — it is *not* a competitive gap. Any "hide the load-use bubble" approach (D.2 in-place rearrange, D.3 NEON-load lever) is **architecturally non-viable on A53** (single LSU, NEON→int transfer adds latency, no speculation past loads). Confirmed by two independent analyses (Gemini D.2/D.3 verdicts).

### 3.5 JIT compile-time overhead in the hot hash path
**Status:** Low (~1.84% of cycles in C++ per E17 region split, 97.89% in JIT-emitted code). The `enableWriting`/`enableExecution` mprotect pair is skipped when RWX is granted (`jit_compiler_a64.cpp:167` `rwx_` flag), which is the on-device default. Not a lever.

## 4. Architectural & Microarchitecture Gaps

From the line-level `jit_compiler_a64_static.S` audit (1329 lines, label map and findings reported verbatim by the explore agent):

### 4.1 NEON / SIMD utilization — **GOOD, mature**
- Hardware AES zero-key form `vaesmcq_u8(vaeseq_u8(s, zero))` is the default (`aes.hpp:99–115`). Adopted 2026-08-03. The earlier 2026-07-20 "AESE incompatible" revert was a misdiagnosis of the *direct* `aese(state, key)` form (AddRoundKey-first = wrong order); zero-key compensation is correct and ships.
- `v2_FE_mix` (static.S:416–492): 48 NEON instructions for 4 AES rounds, keys delivered as `v20–v23` register-resident (no per-round `vld1`). Excellent — no improvements available here.
- Tower-field NEON software AES (`aes.hpp:142–285`, `ARMRX_ENABLE_NEON_AES`) is correctly gated off as the Track-G fallback (−19.4% slower on A53 per AGENTS.md). NEON `vqtbl1q` vtbl tables are 16-entry, which constrains SubBytes via GF(2^4) tower-field isomorphism — clever but loses to hardware AES.
- `v1_FE_mix` (static.S:494–498): 4 EORs, no AES — RandomX v1 RNG path.
- FP scratchpad reads (static.S:243–254) use **8 single `ldr dXX, [x17, #imm]`** asymmetrically against 4 paired `ldp x20, x19` (static.S:250/260/270/280) on the integer side. The explore agent confirms this is **deliberate**: the FP `ldr d` chain fills the 3-cycle `ldp` load-use gap with interleaved `sshll`/`scvtf` work (static.S:262–296). Mature interleave, do not "improve."

### 4.2 PRFM (prefetch) usage — **deliberately minimal, retry is contraindicated**
- Total live PRFM: 7 instructions, all `pldl1keep` (static.S:228, 229, 230, 362, 1008, 1262, 1264).
- **No `pldl2keep` / `pldl3keep` / `pldl3strm` anywhere.** Static.S comment at lines 360–361 acknowledges `pldl2keep` "may be better on systems where L1 scratchpad eviction is measurable in fast mode" — a known but undecided idea.
- **3 PRFMs commented out** at static.S:405–407 (the T21 PRFM-hints experiment, measured +0.26–0.50% cycles, reverted). Verbatim comment: "PRFM should not be re-added in any form."
- **Verdict:** PFM experimentation is closed on evidence for the scratchpad. The single open question is dataset-side `pldl2keep`/`pldl3strm` for fast-mode datasets (multi-MiB, L1/L2-unfriendly) — but the device runs light mode by default (`AGENTS.md` Memory modes), so this is a low-priority target for fast-mode-only deployments.

### 4.3 Cache line / alignment — **GOOD**
- `.p2align 6` at static.S:235 correctly matches A53 I-cache line (64 bytes).
- All scratchpad accesses are 64-byte aligned (static.S:226–227 `lsl #6`). No split-line access in the scratchpad path (verified by the explore agent at lines 254 — last `ldr d, #56` ends within the 64-byte slot).
- `ldp`/`stp` consistently used for paired 16-byte / 32-byte slots (static.S:410–413 int, 503–504 FP, 1027–1030 dataset store, 1310–1318 2way).
- Reversed `ldp x20, x19` destination order (static.S:250/260/270/280, 372/375/378/381) is deliberate — `x19` is consumed first by `eor x5, x5, x19` to match XMRig's AArch64 port; zero-cost on A53 register rename.

### 4.4 Barriers, atomics, acquire-release — **correctly absent**
- **No `dmb`/`dsb`/`isb` anywhere in static.S.** JIT cache maintenance (`__builtin___clear_cache`) is handled in the C++ side (`jit_compiler_a64.cpp:161, 867, 1248`), which emits the appropriate `ic ivau` + `dsb ish` + `isb` sequences under the hood via libgcc.
- **No `ldax`/`stlr`** — plain `ldr`/`str`/`ldp`/`stp`. Correct for the VM (single-threaded per program run); acquire-release would only add cost.
- **No `yield`/`wfe`/`wfi`** — correct (busy-loop miner).
- **No `dc zva` / `dc cvac`** — not used; would not fit RandomX's read-then-write scratchpad pattern. No opportunity.
- Cross-thread ordering (dataset fill → miner) uses C++ `std::memory_order_acquire`/`release` (`mining_engine.cpp:268, 282, 308, 436, 471`) — correct, idiomatic.

### 4.5 Toolchain flags — **GOOD, with one open item**
- `-march=armv8-a+crypto` per-target (`CMakeLists.txt:67`).
- `-fomit-frame-pointer` (except ASAN), `-fvisibility=hidden`, `-fno-semantic-interposition`, `-funroll-loops`, `-frounding-math` (the last is *critical* per AGENTS.md — the VM depends on `fesetround()` runtime changes).
- `-Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic -Wconversion -Wshadow` — strict.
- LTO enabled by default; `ARMRX_DISABLE_LTO=ON` is the GCC 15/16 + musl crash workaround (forced in the cross toolchain `cmake/toolchain-aarch64-musl.cmake:57`).
- **Open item: `ARMRX_FAST_MATH` (−Ofast / −ffast-math) is gated off** because `-ffast-math` would override `-frounding-math` and break the `fesetround()` runtime dependency. The option exists in CMakeLists.txt:133 but is correctly *not* auto-enabled. Do not enable.

### 4.6 **Open: Clang cross-build A/B (Copilot idea #11)**
`perf-tracking.md` Section 3 row 11 notes this is **untried**. GCC 16.1.0 vs GCC 15.2.0 was +7.9% — Clang's codegen for the same source may differ again. Zero risk, ~1 hour to test (build a Clang-compiled cross binary, run KATs, run bench). Recommendation in §6.

## 5. RandomX VM & Memory Optimization Gaps

### 5.1 JIT compiler (`jit_compiler_a64.cpp`)
**Architecture** — well-formed:
- 384-instruction program buffer, 32-word slot/VM-instr worst-case budget (22 KB total per `static.S:308` `.fill`).
- 256-entry engine[] table derived from `instruction_weights.hpp`'s spec-derived frequency weights (single source of truth, shared between JIT and interpreter — `vm.cpp:548`, `jit_compiler_a64.cpp:2113`).
- `IntRegMap[8] = {4,5,6,7,12,13,14,15}` (`jit_compiler_a64.cpp:135`) — leaves x0–x3 for ABI/args, x8 for FPCR, x9–x11 for spMix/literal pool, x16/x17 for scratchpad pointers, x19/x20 for temps, x21–x30 for IMUL_RCP reserves. **Pressure is high but managed.**

**Emitter lookahead scheduler** (`scheduleProgram`, `jit_compiler_a64.cpp:638–739`) — extremely *conservative*:
- Single adjacent-pair swaps only (P→R→Q instead of P→Q→R) with optional distance-3 widening (P→R2→Q→R1).
- Hard barriers: CBRANCH, CFROUND (correct — global FP state mutation / loop replay).
- Hard exclusions: `src==dst` instructions on Q/R (constraint 4, found by stress-test bisection not analysis; mechanism unresolved but necessity established).
- Per-domain CBRANCH "anchors" precomputed (lines 649–664) — never swap against the last writer of a CBRANCH target register. Correct handling of the replay-window problem at the *register-hazard* level (but NOT at the byte-relocation level — see §3.2).
- Superscalar scheduler (`scheduleSuperscalarProgram`, lines 746–775) — no barriers (superscalar programs are straight-line integer-only), but excludes IMUL_RCP from Q/R (correct — IMUL_RCP's pre-pass/sequential-consume literal pool is order-sensitive per the comment block at lines 474–528).

**Code defects / latent issues**:
1. **`emitMovImmediate` and `emitCpoolImmediate`'s fallback paths are byte-identical but separate code paths** (Gemini finding A.4/E.3). If either is changed independently, they could diverge. Latent maintenance hazard.
2. **`num32bitLiterals = 64` pinned at `jit_compiler_a64.cpp:1114`** specifically to *disable* the shared NEON literal-pool path for superscalar C*. The comment explains why (would make superscalar C\* order-sensitive under the scheduler). This is a "load-bearing pin" — if it ever regresses to 0, superscalar immediate loads would silently become order-sensitive. Worth a static_assert or function-level comment.
3. **`ARMRX_MAX_SWAPS` env var** (lines 627–636) is the W3-2 bisection harness — undocumented in any user-facing doc. Harmless.

### 5.2 Huge Pages — **already at parity**
- `virtual_memory.c:235` uses `MAP_HUGETLB | MAP_POPULATE` — XMRig's exact technique.
- E15 (commit `9935734`) confirmed armrx acquires 128×2 MiB as root on device.
- E9 (commit `29aa0ec`) found armrx was NOT getting hugepages initially — root reservation of 256×2 MiB required (`echo 256 | sudo tee /sys/kernel/mm/hugepages/hugepages-2048kB/nr_hugepages`). +0.8% H/s. The reason the win is much smaller than XMRig's "up to 50%" headline: A53's bottleneck is the memory-latency wall (L1D 3cyc / L2 17cyc / DRAM 129ns, single port), not TLB misses.
- **Scratchpad** (`vm.cpp:130–143`) tries `MAP_HUGETLB` 2 MiB (exactly one huge page), falls back to `MAP_ANONYMOUS` + `MADV_HUGEPAGE`, then `MADV_POPULATE_WRITE` (Linux 5.14+) to prefault. Excellent. No gap.

### 5.3 AES hardware acceleration — **adopted, dominant**
- `aes.hpp` zero-key hardware AES for `encrypt_transform`/`decrypt_transform` (gated on `__ARM_FEATURE_AES`, lines 99–115) — adopted 2026-08-03 as default aarch64+crypto path. Item 1 in `residual-gap-optimization-roadmap.md` shipped.
- `aes_hash.cpp` `ARMRX_ENABLE_NEON_TTABLE_AES` (default ON since 2026-08-01) — keeps the scalar T-table *transform* but NEON-vectorizes the AddRoundKey XOR across 4 blocks at once. Saves ~32 instructions per 4-block round on the fill/hash loops.
- **All four AES functions** (`fill_aes_1r_x4`, `fill_aes_4r_x4`, `hash_aes_1r_x4`, `hash_and_fill_aes_1r_x4`, `hash_and_fill_aes_interleaved_x4`) use the NEON-TTABLE path with `vld1q_u8`/`vst1q_u8`/`veorq_u8` for the AddRoundKey batch. Loop keys are loaded once per inner iteration (e.g., `aes_hash.cpp:58–61`). No per-block redundant VLD1.
- **Open: no SVE / SVE2 usage anywhere.** This is *correct* for the target device — MSM8929 is ARMv8.0-A (Cortex-A53), SVE requires ARMv8.6-A+ / SVE-enabled hardware. Not a gap.
- **Open: hardware AES in the JIT-emitted FE_mix block** — already there (static.S:416–492), 3 NEON ops/round. No gap.
- **Open: hardware AES for `aes_generator.cpp` `AesGenerator1R/4R::next`** — the `residual-gap-optimization-roadmap.md` §3 file table lists this as "minor (~0.4M) but same pattern" — unused as a target. Verify adoption.

### 5.4 Scratchpad access — **mature, retry contraindicated**
- Per-worker 2 MiB scratchpad (one huge page when available); 256 KiB L1+L2 fit in A53's 32 KiB L1D + 256–512 KiB L2 (light mode working set is 256 KiB cache + 2 MiB scratchpad).
- Light-mode `rx_calc_dataset_item` is called 16,384×/hash (8 programs × 2048 iterations). The static.S `randomx_calc_dataset_item_aarch64_prefetch` (line 1008) prefetches one cache line via `prfm pldl1keep` immediately before the superscalar mix consumes it. No prefetch-ahead-of-use distance.
- Static.S `randomx_program_aarch64_update_spMix1` (lines 385–407) — the 3 PRFMs for next-iteration scratchpad were **removed** and a verbatim comment forbids re-adding. T21 measured regression.
- Light-mode dataset derivation prologue (static.S:945–989) was specifically reworked 2026-08-03 to **batch the superscalar-constant loads in depth-2 overlap** (x12/x13 alternating — comment at lines 969–974 explains x14–x17 are forbidden because they're live across the JIT call). E12 attempted deeper batching but eor dependency on the load result limited gain to null. **Closed.**

### 5.5 VM execution loop — **single-threaded, no overhead**
- One `jit_->getProgramFunc()(...)` indirect call per hash (`vm.cpp:840`), passing `reg_/mem_regs/scratchpad/iterations=2048`.
- No function call indirection inside the JIT'd loop (entire body is the static.S template + JIT-emitted handler bytes inline).
- `last_mem_regs_` state preserved across `run_execute_only()` (`vm.cpp:861–872`) for `--full-hash-only` bench mode. No issue.

### 5.6 Thread dispatch and pinning — **correct, isolcpus-aware**
- `mining_engine.cpp:356–374` pins workers via `pthread_setaffinity_np` to `core_order_[thread_id % ...]`. `AffinityMode::All` is the default, `AffinityMode::BigOnly` is opt-in.
- isolcpus-aware filtering (`mining_engine.cpp:27–43`, `filter_to_isolated`) keeps only isolated cores when `isolcpus=` is active. Default worker count caps to isolated-core count (audit finding T2-3, committed `3ae8db1`).
- **No NUMA awareness** — but the target device is a single-SoC UMA topology (all cores share one DRAM controller), so NUMA would not help.
- **No `--rt-priority` caucus** beyond `SCHED_FIFO prio 1` with CAP_SYS_NICE warning (`mining_engine.cpp:377–386`).  Incremental contribution on top of isolation is unconfirmed per AGENTS.md.

### 5.7 **Latent issues specific to this audit**
- **SIGINT/Ctrl-C bug (KNOWN, perf-tracking §0 line 71).** `miner_app.cpp:30–32` installs `signal_handler` via `std::signal(SIGINT, signal_handler)` which sets `std::atomic<bool> keep_running{false}`. The handler is **not async-signal-safe** (it calls no async-unsafe functions itself, but the `std::atomic<bool>::store` to a non-lock-free atomic may not be signal-safe by the C++ standard — though on AArch64 it's a single `STR` so likely fine in practice). The user-confirmed bug "Ctrl-C does nothing during pool mining" likely has a different root cause: **the worker threads are stuck inside the JIT'd hash loop (`virtual_machine` call), which is compute-bound and not checking the atomic.** The atomic is checked at the top of `worker_loop`'s `while (running_.load(std::memory_order_relaxed))` (`mining_engine.cpp:428`), but each iteration runs a full RandomX hash (~210 ms) — so the latency to respond can be up to 210 ms. **If the user reports it never responds, the stratum/pool reader thread (`pool_manager` / `stratum_client`) may be blocking SIGINT delivery** — `std::signal`-installed handlers interrupt a thread, but if that thread is blocked in `recv()`/`poll()` with certain signal masks set, the signal may be deferred. **Worth investigating**: install a `sigaction` with `SA_RESTART=0` for SIGINT specifically and ensure the stratum reader thread has SIGINT unblocked, or use `signalfd`/`self-pipe` to wake `poll()`.
- **`MetricsExporter` data race (`docs/archived/plan_completed_phases_1-5.md:192`)** — `server_fd_` read in destructor without fence, written by background thread. Listed as a "one-line, zero-risk fix" but not addressed in current code. TSAN-catchable in narrow window. Not a perf bug but a correctness item.
- **Stale RETROSPECTIVE H/s numbers** (`docs/audits/gemini_full-audit_2026-08-05.md` E.4). Pre-E24 24.8 H/s vs current 26.65; pre-E24 4.84 vs current 5.11. README is updated but RETROSPECTIVE is not.
- **`docs/audits/residual-gap-optimization-roadmap.md`** still recommends "Item 1: hardware AES" — already shipped. Stale.

### Tier 2 — Medium Impact, Medium Effort

**D. E3b: per-opcode JIT-emission diff vs XMRig (the M1-identified lever)** *(expected: identify 1–3 single-opcode fixes that close the 4.6% gap / 1–2 days / medium)*
- M1 localized the gap to instruction-count, not stalls. The systematic comparison (`perf-tracking.md:574` "main-VM memory-op emission ordering vs XMRig") has been **OPEN but not executed** for 4 days.
- Method: same-device, capture both JIT buffers via `--jit-dump` (armrx) and equivalent (XMRig's `--jit-dump` if available, or disassembly of the JIT region in `/proc/<pid>/maps`). Per-opcode tabulation of instruction count.
- Candidates the audit can already flag for inspection:
  - **CBRANCH**: armrx's branchless form (`bne +8; b target`, `jit_compiler_a64.cpp:2033–2038`) is 2 instructions, but expects a backward branch always-taken. RandomX CBRANCH is taken ~0.4% of the time → 99.6% misprediction on the original `beq` (the audit comment at line 2025 acknowledges this). The current branchless form emits ~4–5 instr; XMRig uses 2–3 instr branchy at ~2.4% real miss rate. **The CSEL revert measured a regression per `residual-gap-optimization-roadmap.md` Item 3**, so this is likely already correct to keep — but a fresh instrumented measurement would confirm.
  - **`*_M` consumer op**: armrx emits `add → and → ldr → op` (`emitMemLoad`, `jit_compiler_a64.cpp:1408–1448`); inspect what XMRig emits in the equivalent path. May differ by 1 instr per `*_M` op (×~14% frequency × 16,384 calls = ~2.3M instr/hash delta if true — would explain most of the +4.6M gap).
  - **INEG_R**: armrx emits `sub dst, xzr, dst` (1 instr, `jit_compiler_a64.cpp:1720–1726`); investigate alternatives.
  - **ISWAP_R**: 3-MOV sequence (`jit_compiler_a64.cpp:1812–1830`). XMRig may use a single `mov`+`mov` pair with a temp.

**E. Fix the `MetricsExporter` data race** *(correctness / 30 min / zero perf risk / TSAN-catchable)*
- `metrics.hpp:46, 76` writes `server_fd_` without fence; destructor reads it. One-line `std::atomic<int>` or `memory_order_release/acquire` fix.
- Listed in `docs/archived/plan_completed_phases_1-5.md:192` as "one-line, zero-risk fix" but never applied.

### Tier 3 — High Effort, High Uncertainty

**F. CBRANCH replay-map infrastructure (k3 plan, commit `0e72144` M1→V→C1→D)** *(+2–5% potential / multi-day / medium correctness risk)*
- Build an instruction-index-to-byte-offset map during emission. Modify `h_CBRANCH` to look up replay targets from the map instead of `reg_changed_offset`. Then and only then, enable cross-handler byte relocation for `*_M` to hide the 3-cycle load-use bubble.
- The Gemini audit (D.1, `docs/audits/gemini_full-audit_2026-08-05.md:228`) ranks this #2 but warns: "multi-day implementation with non-trivial correctness risk."
- **Honest assessment:** M1 refuted the premise that `*_M` load-stall is the *competitive* gap (XMRig pays the same stall). So even after this infrastructure lands, the win is bounded by the *asymmetry* between armrx and XMRig, not the absolute stall count. **Diminished ROI** — the case for this is now weaker than when the k3 plan was adopted.
- **Recommendation:** Do not start until D (E3b) is closed. If D finds an emission-count lever, F may be unnecessary.

## Final Verdict

armrx has reached **functional parity with XMRig** on the target device. The 8w 95.2% figure is now best understood (per M1, the most recent measurement) as a *main-VM emit-count* question, not a stall question. The two highest-value next steps are **B (Clang A/B)** and **D (per-opcode emission diff)**, both ≤1–2 days each, both zero correctness risk. The CBRANCH replay-map redesign (F) is technically feasible but the M1 finding weakens its case — defer until D's outcome is known. The codebase's measurement discipline and dead-lead documentation are exemplary and should be preserved as the project's most valuable institutional asset.
